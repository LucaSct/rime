**Clock pinning is withdrawn.** Luca: *"i can fix the clock again but really i dont want to do this

### Everything it produced is saved at `~/rime-perf-harness-m17.8b/`
**(copied out of `/tmp`, which gets wiped between sessions — do not leave it there again).**
- `run_hold.sh`, `hold_series.sh`, `run_one.sh` — the drivers
- `analyze_hold.py`, `analyze.py`, `mde.py`, `clk.py` — the analysis
- `patch_hold.py` — the source patch adding `--hold` / `--hold-out` to the sample
- `h1/` — **8 complete runs**, order `T F F T T F F T`, `--frames 600 --hold 1500`
- `h2/` — **6 of 8 runs**, order `F T T F F T T F`, taken deliberately under real contention.
  Incomplete: the series was killed when the session stopped.

### The design (worth keeping — this is the valuable output)
- **Do not pin the clocks; filter on them.** Each run records a 200 ms `nvidia-smi` trace (`.clk`
  sidecar); the analysis keeps only frames whose nearest sample has **both** domains at boost
  (`clocks.gr >= 1700`, `clocks.mem >= 7000`). Dropped frames are counted, never silently discarded.
- **A render-only `--hold` loop after the measured frames.** This is what makes the card boost at all
  (the sim-bound frame is why the governor parks it) and stops a 0.13 ms effect drowning in 15 ms of
  physics.
- **Per-arm cooked-directory COPIES** (`cooked_T`, `cooked_F`) instead of moving `manifest.txt` in
  the live tree — a failed restore can no longer mis-attribute a run. It also built the brick in its
  own git worktree; the main tree was never touched (verified).
- **Contention recorded per run** (`.cpu` sidecar): `/proc/stat` each second minus `the_block`'s own
  jiffies, giving a `foreign_max`. This is the guard the lost script lacked, and the only way to
  distinguish "abandoned for contention" from "fine" on a sample that **always exits 1** on the
  budget gate.
- **Paired design with an empirical null**: the interleaved order yields both A/B adjacent pairs and
  same-arm A/A adjacent pairs, so the A/B delta is judged against a noise floor measured on the same
  box in the same sitting.
- Per-pass attribution is retained (`forward-pbr shadowed`, `ssr-resolve`, `depth-prepass`,
  `tonemap`, `passes_sum`).

### Two concerns to settle BEFORE trusting any number out of it
1. **The clock guard may select differently per arm.** Filtering on GPU boost conditions the sample
   on GPU activity, and GPU activity is what the treatment changes. If the textured arm boosts more
   readily, the surviving frame populations differ and their medians are not comparable. **Check the
   retained/dropped counts per arm first.** This is the shape recorded in
   `capped-metrics-cannot-see-their-own-cap`.
2. **The hold loop measures something narrower than ADR-0041 currently claims.** A render-only frame
   is not a gameplay frame. It supports *"this is the GPU cost of the ground's material"*; it does
   **not** support the ADR's stronger existing claim that the cost *"reaches the frame"*. If the
   re-take is hold-only, that paragraph must be requalified, not merely re-numbered.

Also note the proposal **costs a source change** — `--hold`/`--hold-out` become a measurement-only
mode in the sample.

## 3. Water / fire / smoke track — planning, requested but NOT delivered

Luca asked for a water + fire + particle/smoke track, three bricks planned by Fable, grounded in
Disney/ETH research, "highly advanced simulation techniques". *"I love to watch smoke and want to
see it in the engine."* **The planning agent was killed before it reported. Nothing from it exists.**

### What I established directly from the repo (keep this — it reframes the task)
All three domains **already have designated homes and deferred plans**. Do not plan a parallel
structure.
- **`engine/vfx` exists today** — a deliberate CPU billboard STUB from M8.4 whose header says "the
  real GPU-driven FX system (track fx1) replaces this whole module". Its `Family` enum already has
  `ImpactDust`, **`LingeringSmoke`**, `MuzzleFlash`.
- **fx1a landed** as m13.1 — `render::FxParticlePass`, additive billboards, gated.
- **fx1b — "the compute-sim scale-up" — was specified and CUT**, not forgotten. ADR-0035 §5 made it
  "contingent on the ledger" and put it on the cut list (`audio → fx1b → …`). **Brick 1 of the smoke
  track is plausibly just un-cutting fx1b**, and should be named in continuity with it.
- **`docs/ROADMAP.md` ~line 1630** defines both tracks, and already answers the "common base"
  question *differently from how Luca framed it*:
  - **Track FX** (`engine/vfx`) — "a GPU particle substrate with **fire and dust/smoke** as effect
    families"; fire drives lights, smoke reads the M10 lighting data; **fire-as-light deferred behind
    its seam**.
  - **Track FL** (`engine/fluids`) — "**CPU heightfield water** with two-way buoyancy coupling".
    **Decided NO at M12.0** (ADR-0035 §5, reaffirmed ADR-0036 §104). ADR-0026:142 keeps the seams.

### The unresolved design question (this is the real decision)
The repo pairs **fire + smoke** on one particle substrate and gives **water** a heightfield — a
different substrate sharing no machinery. Luca's intuition is that **water + smoke** share a base,
which is the hybrid particle-grid answer (PIC/FLIP/**APIC**, and **MPM** above it). Both are
defensible and mutually exclusive:
- *Heightfield water*: cheap, ships, couples to buoyancy trivially — but 2.5D; cannot pour, splash or
  fill a room, and shares nothing with smoke.
- *Hybrid particle-grid*: unifies water/smoke/fire/granular, is what "highly advanced" points at —
  but far more expensive, and **reverses a ratified roadmap entry, so it needs an ADR**.

### Hard constraint any plan must answer
**The frame is already over budget**: `frame` p99 **19.849** against a ratified **16.600**;
`sim.block` p99 **15.468** against a **6.000** budget. ADR-0041 Ruling 1 is "the budget is earned
before the bar is spent". A fluid solver planned into that frame without an explicit answer for where
its cost comes from will be rejected.

### Established repo facts (verified, don't re-derive)
- **The RHI already exposes compute** — `ComputePipeline`, `dispatch`, `StorageBuffer`,
  `StorageImage` in `engine/rhi/include/`, and 5 `.comp` shaders already exist (e.g.
  `cluster_cull.comp`). Compute is **not** a gap.
- Lighting available to couple to: clustered, SSR, sky, SDF, DDGI. **There is no volumetric fog /
  froxel pass** — if smoke wants volumetric lighting rather than billboards, that is a real new cost.

### Literature, citations verified independently (not recalled)
- **Wavelet Turbulence for Fluid Simulation** — Kim, Thürey, James, Gross, SIGGRAPH 2008,
  `10.1145/1360612.1360649`. ETH/Disney Zurich; Academy Technical Achievement Award. Adds
  high-frequency detail as post-process on a cheap low-res sim, **no linear solve, parallelises
  trivially** — the best-shaped technique for a 60 Hz budget.
- **Two-Scale Particle Simulation** — Solenthaler & Gross, SIGGRAPH 2011, `10.1145/1964921.1964976`.
- **Data-driven Fluid Simulations using Regression Forests** — Ladický, Jeong, Solenthaler,
  Pollefeys, Gross, SIGGRAPH Asia 2015, `10.1145/2816795.2818129` (became Physics Forests, real-time).
- **The Affine Particle-In-Cell Method (APIC)** — Jiang, Schroeder, Selle, Teran, Stomakhin,
  TOG 34(4), 2015, `10.1145/2766996`.
- **Augmented MPM for phase-change and varied materials** — Stomakhin, Schroeder, Jiang, Chai, Teran,
  Selle, TOG 33(4) art.138, 2014, `10.1145/2601097.2601176`. Water, melting and heat transport **on
  one solver** — this is literally the "common base" Luca guessed at.
