# ADR-0056: the replacement-DAG cook, and payload v2's LOD sphere

- Status: Accepted
- Date: 2026-09-30
- Amends: [ADR-0043](0043-virtualized-geometry-m18.md) gate 3 ("cooked replacement groups") — this
  is the cook that gate names. Everything else in ADR-0043 and ADR-0044 stands.

## Context

Until M18.6 the Rust cooker wrote only a permanently resident leaf level; every multi-level
hierarchy the renderer had been tested on was hand-built. ADR-0043 asks for a checked acyclic
*replacement DAG*: a complete parent representation replacing a complete set of children, so that
no mixed-LOD boundary can crack.

The Nanite construction (Karis et al., 2021) is the known answer: group neighbouring clusters, lock
each group's outer boundary, simplify the group to half, re-split, and re-group the new clusters so
the seams move every level. Two things about our existing contract shaped how it is written down:

1. **The selectors walk a tree.** `select_virtual_geometry()` pushes every child of a refined group
   and has no visited set; its GPU twin derives one parent per group ("first parent wins"). In a
   true Nanite DAG one simplification's outputs have *all* of its inputs as children, so a group
   shared by several parents would be selected once per refining parent — duplicates on the CPU,
   and exponentially many through a deep graph.
2. **The payload had no per-group bounds.** The monotone bounding sphere a view-dependent selector
   needs (and that makes Nanite's cut consistent under per-group distances) had nowhere to live.

## Decision

**The cook is `asset_pipeline::virtual_geometry_dag` (`Asset::from_mesh_dag`), and the Rust writer
stays the writer of record.** Per level: spatial 128-triangle single-material clusters (region
growing over shared-edge adjacency in Morton order); a greedy shared-edge-weighted partition into
groups of ~8 (4–32 after merging small groups); every vertex shared with another group, on a
border or non-manifold edge, on an attribute or material seam, or on a non-disc fan is locked; QEM
half-edge collapse to 50% (link condition, duplicate-face and fold-over rejection, attributes taken
unmodified from real source vertices); re-split. It stops at one cluster, at `MAX_LEVELS`, or when
a level removes <5% of triangles *and* no clusters — that level is discarded and the stop reason
and count are recorded in `DagStats`, beside counters for every rejected collapse, locked vertex,
merged/kept small group and fully locked group. No dependency was added.

**Each simplification ("swap unit") is written so a tree walk realises it atomically.** One
payload group per cluster; each output cluster of a unit is the single parent of a disjoint,
non-empty share of the unit's input clusters. That share always exists because every cluster but a
material's last is exactly full, so a simplification — which never adds triangles — never yields
more clusters than it consumed (a violation is reported as `LeafCookError::DagInvariant`, never
written). The top-level clusters form the one permanently resident root group. One page per swap
unit holds its inputs and depends on the pages holding its outputs. With monotone errors, the
selector's single projected threshold, and that dependency rule, either every output of a unit
refines or none does — so the unit swaps whole, as in Nanite, while every group is reached along
exactly one path. No change to `engine/render` was needed, and the GPU twin agrees with the CPU
oracle on the cooked asset.

**Errors are cumulative and measured geometrically.** A unit's error is its own simplification
error plus the largest error it inherits (the triangle inequality makes the sum a bound against the
source; the sum also makes parent ≥ child hold by construction). Its own error is the largest
distance from a collapsed-away vertex to the triangles that absorbed it. QEM only orders collapses:
the square root of an accumulated quadric grows with the number of merged planes and overstated a
unit sphere's root by roughly 10x in the first draft.

**Payload v2 appends a 16-byte LOD sphere (centre, radius) to each group record.** Appending keeps
v1's prefix unchanged; the C++ reader accepts v1 (spheres read as zero) and v2. Each unit's sphere
encloses its inputs' spheres, and radii are computed in f64 from the f32-rounded centre and rounded
up, so containment holds exactly. `validate_virtual_geometry()` now rejects any replacement edge
whose child error exceeds its parent's or whose child sphere escapes its parent's
(`NonMonotoneGroups`), within a 2^-20 relative guard. The RMA1 schema hash is unchanged: the
payload's own version field is the versioning mechanism ADR-0043 set up.

Rejected: adding a visited set to `select_virtual_geometry()` and letting outputs share children.
It is a small change, but it is `engine/render`'s code, it would make the CPU oracle and the GPU
twin's first-parent rule disagree on shared groups, and the single-parent encoding costs nothing
the runtime can see.

## Consequences

The pipeline has a real multi-level cook: a 16,128-triangle sphere halves exactly per level to one
126-triangle cluster in 7 levels, and 19 of its 33 units feed more than one next-level group (it
is a DAG, not a tree). Proofs, all on the cooked bytes: byte determinism; monotone errors and
spheres on every edge; bit-identical boundaries for every swap unit; and valid, unique, watertight
cuts over a threshold x partial-residency sweep in Rust and, through the real C++ reader, selector
and GPU twin, on the committed fixture `tests/assets/fixtures/vg_dag_sphere.rvg`.

Costs and open edges:

- **Pages are one per swap unit, and dependencies are direct.** The selector's dependency walk has
  no visited set, so a deep DAG re-walks shared ancestor pages; fine at fixture scale, worth
  memoising before large assets stream. Page sizing and packing belong to M18.5.
- **The selector does not read the LOD sphere yet.** It still uses one conservative
  pixels-per-metre per instance; per-group distance evaluation is the next consumer.
- **Seams and material boundaries are locked at every level.** A mesh whose top level is all seam
  stops on "no progress" with a larger root; a two-material sphere's root is two flat caps with an
  honest 1.25 m error. Border collapse along an open edge is not implemented.
- The greedy partitioner is not METIS; group shapes are good enough to halve a sphere every level
  but are not claimed optimal.
