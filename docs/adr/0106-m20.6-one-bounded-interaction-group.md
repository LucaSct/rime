# ADR-0106: m20.6 — one bounded interaction group: the player pushes the crate

- Status: Accepted (owner, 2026-10-25: keep ADR-0104's order — the interaction group is next, before
  multi-client and `host`)
- Date: 2026-10-25
- Extends: ADR-0104 (the predicted set), ADR-0105 (speculative spawn)

## Context

ADR-0104's staged path puts **one bounded interaction group** at m20.6: the client predicts, besides its own
player and its own projectile, one nearby dynamic object it acts on, so the local response to a push is
immediate rather than a round trip late. hello-game has exactly one candidate. Its player is a kinematic
capsule and its crate is the world's only dynamic body (`samples/hello-game/game.cpp:298-307`), and a
kinematic body is never pushed back, so a crate error can never corrupt the player. The projectile has no
physics at all, so letting it knock the crate would be a new rule tied to a speculative spawn; that waits.

Scoping (Opus 5.5 with web research, `reports/rime-oct25-m20.6-interaction/out.md`) checked the physics:
`PhysicsWorld::step` advances the whole world and cannot step a subset (`engine/physics/include/rime/physics/world.hpp:356-365`),
its contact cache is warm-started and survives a teleport (`world.hpp:339-343`), and `PhysicsSync` keeps a
private last-pushed pose that it differentiates into a velocity (`sync.hpp:60-70`). Unity Netcode for
Entities predicts physics the same way proposed below (a separate prediction physics world); Source does not
predict pushed props; Photon Quantum's whole-world rollback is what ADR-0104 already declined.

## Decision

### 1. The group is the crate, while the player is near it

Only the player-pushes-crate interaction. Damage, hits and score stay authoritative (ADR-0104 §2).

### 2. The server declares membership, with hysteresis

The server owns the roster and the epoch (ADR-0104 §1), so it decides. The crate **joins** when it is within
contact distance plus the player's top speed × the 64-tick replay budget (≈ 5.1 m in hello-game), and
**leaves** only beyond that radius + 1 m AND after 30 ticks at rest and untouched — so it never leaves while
moving. On the join tick the client seeds the crate **from the checkpoint and replays**, never from the
interpolated mirror (a past pose with no velocity). Every join, leave and refused join is counted.

### 3. A small prediction physics world, rebuilt on every restore

No engine physics change. The client keeps a separate prediction physics world (floor, kinematic player,
crate). Three rules make it replayable: it is **rebuilt from the checkpoint on every restore** (so no
contact-cache or sleep state leaks across a correction); the player body is **driven directly** with
`set_body_state`, never through `PhysicsSync`'s remembered pose (which would invent a velocity the size of
the correction and fire the crate off); and **sleeping is off** in it. Consequence accepted: the rebuilt world
does not carry the server's warm-start cache, so client and server crates are not bit-identical even with no
disturbance — corrections are expected, small, and measured.

### 4. Correct the simulation, smooth the presentation

The simulation always takes the checkpoint and replays; it never blends. Presentation keeps a per-member
visual error offset OUTSIDE rewindable state (like ADR-0105's ledger) that decays with τ ≈ 100 ms and snaps
above 1 m. Raw (simulation) and presented jumps are measured separately. The raw bound is derived from the
game, not the data: `(v_push_max + J_max / m) · H · dt` = (4 m/s + 2 m/s) · 64 / 60 ≈ 6.4 m, where `J_max` is
the largest server-only impulse the game declares (a 12 N·s kick on the 6 kg crate).

### 5. The proof

`hello_game_play_interaction`: two processes, GPU-free, with delay, loss and jitter. A tape that starts in
the group, retreats until the crate leaves, returns until it joins, pushes it, takes a **server-only kick**
while it is a member, rests until it leaves, takes a second kick as a non-member and is re-joined while
sliding. Gates: equal non-zero digests differing from a no-push control; crate response ≤ 1 tick predicted
vs ≥ the round trip without; joins/leaves exactly as scripted with no join while in contact; the
undisturbed residual measured and bounded (its value and cause reported, not assumed); the kick shows a
correction > 10 cm, converges within 2 checkpoints and stays under the raw bound; presented jumps bounded by
the decay. Controls that must fail: presenting the mirror, restoring the crate from a stale checkpoint, not
rebuilding the prediction world, joining on contact, leaving while moving.

### 6. Bricks

m20.6a, a pure leaf (membership policy with hysteresis, the correction smoother, their counters); m20.6b, the
wiring (server membership + crate state with velocity in the checkpoint, the client prediction physics world
and its three rules, a replay-twice-identical test); m20.6c, the two-process proof with `--interaction-fault`
and `--interaction-debug-break`.

## Consequences

- hello-game's checkpoint carries the crate's velocity and the roster grows to {player, projectile?, crate?}.
- The biggest risk is physics state the checkpoint does not capture; §3's three rules and the replay-twice
  test exist for it, and a final-digest check cannot see it.
- Not here: projectile–crate contact, more than one predicted dynamic object, contention between clients
  (needs multi-client), the multi-client/`host` design (scoped in `reports/rime-oct25-m20.6-scope/out.md`).
