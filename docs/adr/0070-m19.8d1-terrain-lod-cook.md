# ADR-0070 (m19.8d1): The terrain LOD chain, cooked — nested subsampling and a level-aware world manifest

- Status: Accepted
- Date: 2026-10-06
- Number: provisional; 0068 is m19.8c's (on its own branch). Renumber at merge if it clashes.

## Context

ADR-0069 (m19.8a) streams level-0 terrain tiles into a fixed number of GPU slots. A tile that
is wanted but not resident is a **hole**, and every distant tile costs the same as a near one.
M19 ends with seamless travel (Luca, m19.8a spec), so M19.8d adds a level-of-detail chain in
three bricks:

1. **8d1 (this brick):** cook the chain, and make the world manifest level-aware;
2. **8d2:** render it: quadtree selection, 2:1 balance, stitched edges, children geomorphing onto
   the parent's triangulation, parent fallback instead of holes, a pinned root cover;
3. **8d3:** bake each parent's appearance (its palette).

Collision stays on level 0 only (ADR-0068) and is not touched.

An outside consult (`gpt-6-astra`, read-only) proposed the overall scheme: geomipmapped patches
in a quadtree; children morphing onto the parent's **triangulated** surface, never a bilinear
one; the cook emitting separately addressable parents with conservative descendant bounds and
geometric-error bounds; nested integer subsampling under one quantisation; dyadic alignment
validated; coarse cover never needing its descendants loaded. Its items 2 and 5 are what this
brick builds. Each was treated as a hypothesis and checked against ADR-0060, ADR-0069 and the code.

## Decision

### 1. Levels by nested integer subsampling, not filtering

A level-L tile at (x, z) covers the 2^L × 2^L level-0 tiles starting at (x·2^L, z·2^L). It has the
**same** sample count N as a level-0 tile and 2^L times the spacing. Its samples are **exactly
every second sample of its four level-(L−1) children**: the integers are copied, with no
averaging and no re-quantisation. So every parent vertex is a child vertex, bit for bit.

Why not a low-pass filter, the way image mip-maps are built? A mip texel is never drawn on top of
its children. A terrain parent is: 8d2 morphs a child onto the parent as the camera recedes, and
stitches a fine tile to a coarse neighbour along their shared edge. Both are exact only when the
coarse vertices are a subset of the fine ones. The morph then only slides the child's *extra*
vertices onto the parent's triangles, and a stitched edge shares every coarse vertex. A filtered
parent puts its vertices at heights no child has, so every LOD boundary would crack and need a
skirt. The cost is aliasing: a one-sample spike survives or vanishes depending on its parity. The
geometric error (§2) measures that aliasing, so 8d2 refines exactly where it bites.

Consequences of this rule:

- **(N − 1) must be even.** Taking every second sample lands on a child's last sample only if the
  child has an even number of cells. The cook and the manifest both refuse an odd count once a
  world has parents. 2^k + 1 samples satisfies every level at once, because each level is built
  from children with the same N.
- **One quantisation per world**, shared by every level. Then a parent's integer and a child's
  integer at the same spot are directly comparable, and the coincident-sample check (§4) is an
  integer compare.
- **Spacing doubles exactly.** The cook writes a parent's extent as `size × 2^L`, so its payload
  spacing `size·2^L / (N−1)` is bit-identical to `(size / (N−1)) · 2^L`: scaling by a power of two
  commutes with IEEE rounding. The engine compares spacing **bit for bit** at every level.
- **ADR-0060's fixed diagonal holds at every level, unchanged.** A parent is an ordinary
  heightfield.

### 2. Per-tile LOD metadata lives in the world manifest, not the heightfield payload

- `min_y` / `max_y` bound **every level-0 sample beneath the tile**, not only the tile's own
  samples. A parent that skipped a peak must still be culled as tall as that peak. The cook
  takes the min and max of the children's integer bounds recursively, which equals the extremes
  of all descendant level-0 samples. It converts them to metres in the engine's own f32 order,
  `origin.y + (offset + scale·q)`, so the bound holds in the engine's arithmetic.
- `geometric_error` is in metres. It is the maximum, over the level-0 samples beneath the tile,
  of |true height − the tile's own triangulated surface at that x, z|. The surface uses the fixed
  diagonal with barycentric interpolation, never bilinear. The arithmetic is exact: a level-0
  sample at offset p sits at tile-sample position p / 2^L, a dyadic rational, so the barycentric
  height in quantisation steps is exact in f64. The cook then converts to metres and **rounds up**
  to f32, so the value stays a bound. Level 0 is 0.

**Where they live.** They go in the manifest's tile line, not the heightfield payload, for three
reasons:

- they describe a tile's *relation to the tiles beneath it*, which a heightfield does not know;
- a renderer needs them *before* deciding to load the tile;
- the payload stays unchanged: parents are plain v1 heightfields, every reader and fixture is
  untouched, and no payload version is bumped.

The heightfield's own `min_sample`/`max_sample` are still the tile's own sample range, which the
reader uses as an integrity check. A parent is usable with none of its descendants loaded.

### 3. The cook entry point: `rime terrain-world <name>.terrainworld.toml`

The description uses the `*.terrainlayer.toml` style: a strict, hand-parsed TOML subset, with
unknown and duplicate keys refused. It has `levels = N` (1..=17; 1 means no parents) and one
`tile_<x>_<z> = "source"` per level-0 tile. Each source is a `rime heightfield` source with its
own sidecar. One key per tile means a coordinate named twice is a duplicate key.

The cook outputs:

- every level-0 tile, cooked exactly as `rime heightfield` cooks it (splat included);
- every parent, as a v1 heightfield;
- `<name>.terrainworld`, the manifest, with tiles named `<name>_L<level>_<x>_<z>.rhf`.

**Refusals.** All of them are collected, counted by kind and explained in one error, rather than
stopping at the first:

| kind | meaning |
| --- | --- |
| `NotSquare` / `SizeMismatch` / `SpacingMismatch` / `QuantisationMismatch` | a tile disagrees with the first, bit for bit |
| `OddCellCount` | (N − 1) odd with `levels > 1` |
| `PlacementMismatch` | origin off the grid (± 1 mm, the engine's tolerance) |
| `BorderMismatch` | edge neighbours disagree on shared samples (see below) |
| `Misaligned` | the world's bounding box does not start on a multiple of the root span 2^(levels−1) |
| `IncompleteRootCover` | the bounding box is not a whole number of root spans: roots cannot cover it without padding, and the cook does not pad |
| `MissingChild` | a 2×2 block at some level has one to three of its children. No flat fill. |

The border check is not optional. A parent takes **one** copy of a shared edge, so if the two
copies differed, the other child would not coincide with its parent. Checking borders is how the
cook *guarantees* the coincidence invariant rather than merely hoping for it.

**Root cover.** A world that is not a power-of-two square gets a **root grid**: a 4×2 world with
`levels = 2` has two roots. The root level must still cover the whole world. Empty root blocks
*between* covered ones (a world of islands) are allowed, because every tile still has its chain.

### 4. `TerrainWorld` becomes level-aware

- **Keys.** `TerrainTileKey` is (level, coord). Its constructor is not aggregate, so m19.8a's
  `find({1, 0})` still means "level-0 tile (1, 0)" and is not ambiguous. Parents are found by
  floor division (`terrain_parent_coord`, an arithmetic shift), so tiles −1 and 0 have different
  parents.
- **Manifest line.** The new tile line has 10 fields:
  `tile level x z revision min_y max_y geometric_error id path`. m19.8a's 8-field line reads as
  level 0 with error 0. `tiles()`, `find(coord)` and `tiles_within` stay level 0, so the m19.8a
  streamer and all its tests are unchanged.
- **Shape validation** (`validate_levels`, run by `parse`) checks three things:
  - every parent has its four children (`missing_children`);
  - every tile below the top level has a parent (`uncovered_tiles`), so the top level covers the
    whole world;
  - (N − 1) is even (`odd_lod_samples`).

  A failing chain is **dropped as a unit**: level 0 stands, and the counters say why. A partial
  chain would give 8d2 parents it can neither trust nor fall back on.
- **Content validation** needs the payloads:
  - `check_tile(grid, key, asset)` checks spacing = grid × 2^L bit for bit, the grid's one
    quantisation, and the dyadic origin. A new `AlignmentMismatch` covers a parent sitting a whole
    number of level-0 tiles off its dyadic corner, i.e. built from a block that started on the
    wrong child. It is told apart from an ordinary `PlacementMismatch`.
  - `samples_coincide(parent, child)` checks that every parent sample over the child equals the
    child's sample there.
  - `verify_lod_chain(world, load, refusals)` loads each tile, runs `check_tile`, and runs both
    checks for every parent/child pair. The loader is a callback, so the check owns no IO policy.
    It reports `tiles_checked` and `pairs_checked` (guardrail 5: a verifier that cannot see what it
    skipped still reads as passing). 8d2's streamer runs the same two checks per pair as payloads
    arrive.
- **Counters.** Every refusal is counted. New `TerrainWorldRefusals` fields:
  `alignment_mismatches`, `odd_lod_samples`, `missing_children`, `uncovered_tiles`,
  `coincidence_mismatches`, `bad_levels`, `unloadable_tiles`.

### 5. Parent appearance is out of scope

Parents carry no palette. Their appearance is 8d3's coarse bake, because a parent can span tiles
with different palettes, and their union need not fit four layers.

## A choice beyond the spec: the cooked error is SATURATED

The spec defines `geometric_error` as the raw maximum deviation (§2), and its proof (b) asks for
the error to be monotone non-decreasing up the chain. **The raw deviation is not monotone.** A
coarse surface can pass closer to a spike than a finer one does. Measured on the 4×4 bumpy test
world (9×9 samples, 3 levels): **1 of the 5 parents** has a raw deviation below one of its
children's.

A refine-when-too-coarse selection needs a parent's error to dominate its children's. Otherwise
it can accept a parent as good enough over a child it would have refined (the glossary's
*monotone LOD error*, which ADR-0059's cluster DAG relies on too). So the cooked value is

    error(tile) = max(raw deviation(tile), max over its children of error(child))

This is still an upper bound on the tile's own deviation, and it equals the raw deviation
wherever the chain is already monotone. Proof (b) checks both branches against an independent
brute force, and asserts that the saturation is load-bearing on its world.

## Proofs

Rust: `tools/asset-pipeline/src/terrain_world.rs` tests and the `lod_world` fixture test. C++:
`tests/assets/terrain_lod_test.cpp`.

- **(a) Nesting exactness.** On a 4×4 world of hashed "random" heights (9×9 samples, root at
  level 2), every parent sample equals both the global level-0 sample at that position and the
  level-(L−1) child sample there. C++: `samples_coincide` holds for all four children, and a
  doctored sample breaks exactly the children it lies over.
- **(b) Geometric error.**
  - It is 0 on a planar world.
  - On a bumpy world it is positive and monotone, and it equals a brute force that uses different
    maths: world-space positions in metres, with the height read off the triangle's *plane* (a
    cross-product normal) instead of a sample-space interpolation. Equal means "rounded up, and
    the f32 just below is ≤ the brute force".
  - C++ recomputes the Rust-cooked fixture parent's error from the payloads. That is an
    independent measurement in another language, and it agrees to the f32 rounding.
- **(c) Bounds.** min/max bound every descendant level-0 sample and are attained. The test also
  asserts that a parent's own samples differ from those extremes somewhere, so (c)'s falsification
  is not vacuous.
- **(d) Refusals**, each counted: odd (N−1), quantisation, spacing, size, not square, missing child
  (at level 1, and one level up when a whole block is absent), incomplete root cover, misaligned,
  placement, border. In C++: missing child, uncovered tiles, a skipped level, odd samples,
  `bad_levels`, spacing/quantisation/alignment/placement at a level, coincidence, unloadable.
- **(e) m19.8a is unchanged.** All 8a asset tests and the 46 terrain-render tests are green on both
  GPUs. A mixed manifest of 8- and 10-field lines parses.
- **Cross-language.** `tests/assets/fixtures/lod_world*` is a 2×2 world with one parent, cooked by
  `rime terrain-world` and pinned byte for byte by the Rust fixture test. C++ verifies it end to
  end: 5 tiles, 4 pairs, ok.

**Falsified, each restored:**

| sabotage | result |
| --- | --- |
| min/max from the parent's own samples | (c) red |
| bilinear instead of the fixed-diagonal triangle | (b) red: cooked 48.3595 m vs brute 48.9645 m; the planar case stays green, as it must |
| skip `samples_coincide` in the verifier | the doctored chain is accepted: `v.ok()` and `coincidence_mismatches == 1` red |

## Consequences

- A world can be cooked with its full LOD chain, and the engine can read, shape-check and
  content-check it. Nothing draws parents yet (8d2).
- The cook validates borders, so a mismatched level-0 world is now refused at cook time instead of
  being order-dependently refused at load time (ADR-0069's known limit, for cooked worlds).
- `TerrainWorldTile` grew `level` and `geometric_error`, placed after `path` so existing aggregate
  initialisers keep their meaning.

## Known limits

- **Cook cost:** each level visits every level-0 sample once to measure its error, so the cook is
  O(levels × world samples). It is unmeasured at real world sizes. The cook is offline.
- **`verify_lod_chain` loads each tile about twice.** That is fine for a tool or a test; the
  streamer will check pairs incrementally instead.
- The manifest's `revision` is written as 0. 8c owns its meaning.
- **The error is a vertical distance.** It does not account for a slope's horizontal error. 8d2
  projects it as a screen-space bound.
- **Parents have no appearance** (8d3) and **no collision** (by design, ADR-0068).
