# ADR-0072 (m19.8d3): Baked appearance for coarse terrain tiles, faded by the geometry's own morph factor

- Status: Accepted
- Date: 2026-10-06
- Number: provisional (0068 is m19.8c's, on its own branch). Renumber at merge if it clashes.

## Context

ADR-0071 (m19.8d2) draws the LOD chain: coarse tiles where fine ones are not selected or not
resident, each tile geomorphing onto its parent so a switch moves no vertex. It left parents drawn
with the flat placeholder material and counted every such draw (`fallback_appearance_draws`),
because a parent has no splat palette (ADR-0070 §5: the level-0 tiles beneath it may carry
different palettes, and their union need not fit four layers). So the geometry no longer popped,
and the colour did: a hillside turned from grass and gravel to grey the frame it was merged.

This brick gives parents an appearance and removes that pop. The design was decided in the
brick's spec; this ADR records it as built, the choices made within and beyond it, and what was
measured.

## Decision

### 1. Bake offline, in `rime terrain-world`

Every parent tile whose level-0 tiles all carry a palette gets an **appearance bake**: the
level-0 appearance, averaged. It is asked for with one key in the description,
`palette_dir = "<dir>"`, naming the directory of the palettes' **cooked** assets (materials,
terrain layers and their textures). The cook indexes that directory by content hash and resolves
each palette slot the way the engine's `TerrainLayerBuilder` does (ADR-0066 §2): a `Material`
gives base colour, metallic and roughness; a `TerrainLayer` gives its material's scalars, tinted
by its texture's mean albedo, and `height_contrast × mean height` as its effective height.
Without the key the cook, its files and its manifest are m19.8d1's, byte for byte.

*(A choice beyond the spec.)* The spec said to bake with each layer's mean albedo and did not say
where the cook learns it: a heightfield's palette names layers by AssetId only. Reading the
cooked assets — rather than adding the layers' sources to the description — keeps the terrain
naming "exactly the bytes it needs", needs no second path to the same data, and makes the bake
see precisely the texels the GPU will sample.

**The level-0 appearance of a sample** is `terrain.frag`'s m19.7c (fix 1) blend, evaluated on the
CPU at the sample: the splat weights sampled bilinearly, corner-aligned as the shader samples
them; `height_blend` with the layers' effective heights (the same bypass); then the
difference-form blend of colour, metallic and roughness. Each layer is reduced to constants — its
**mean** albedo and **mean** height. *Detail below the parent's texel spacing is averaged away by
design*: a layer's pattern, and the way its height map breaks up a transition, do not survive.
That is the right content for a tile drawn too far away to resolve them, and it is a real
difference from the level-0 picture, which §3's fade crosses gradually.

### 2. One texel per vertex, a footprint in world sample space, linear light

**Vertex-aligned texels.** A bake is N × N texels for a tile of N × N samples: texel (i, j) *at*
sample (i, j), not N − 1 texels centred on cells. Sampling at a vertex returns that vertex's
texel untouched by any filter weight; a child's vertex (i, j) sits at parent sample
((offset + i)/2, (offset + j)/2) — on a texel when even, exactly midway between two when odd, the
lattice the geometry morph uses; and the texels along an edge two tiles share describe the same
world samples, so they can be made identical.

**The footprint.** A level-L texel averages the level-0 samples within half a parent cell of it:
global sample offsets −2^(L−1) … +2^(L−1) on each axis, the two end samples at half weight (a box
one parent cell wide). It is a function of the texel's **global** position only and reads
whichever level-0 tiles hold those samples, across the parent's own border. So a texel on an edge
two parents share has the same footprint from both sides and the two bakes carry the same bytes
there. Two details make that literal: a sample on a level-0 tile border is held by two or four
tiles whose splat maps need not agree, and its value is the **mean over the tiles that hold it**;
a sample no tile holds (beyond the world's border, or over a palette-less tile) is dropped and the
weights renormalised — the "clamp" at the world border, the same from either side.

**Linear light.** Every average — the layer's mean albedo, the footprint — is taken in linear
light and encoded to sRGB once, at the end. sRGB bytes are not proportional to light; averaging
them darkens every mix (a black/white checker is byte 128 averaged as bytes, byte 188 as light).
The level-0 pixels a parent replaces are filtered in linear light by the GPU, so a bake averaged
in sRGB would be visibly darker than the ground it stands for.

### 3. Format

Two single-level `.rtex` textures per baked parent, named on its manifest line (four appended
fields: colour id, colour path, material id, material path — a 14-field line; level-0 lines and
unbaked parents keep 10):

- `<tile>_bake_color.rtex` — RGBA8 **sRGB**, A = 255;
- `<tile>_bake_material.rtex` — RGBA8 **UNORM**, R = metallic, G = roughness, B = 0, A = 255.

Two textures rather than one packed RGBA8: the colour wants the sRGB transfer (the 8 bits spent
where banding shows), and an sRGB format leaves only one linear channel for two scalars.

**No mip chain.** A tile is drawn with one texel per vertex at every distance it is selected for;
a farther camera selects a coarser tile, which *is* the mip. The texture reader therefore accepts
exactly one level as well as a full chain (`decode_texture`); anything in between is still
refused. A 257² tile's bake is 528 KB.

### 4. Draw

`TerrainPass::set_bake` gives a tile two owned textures. A LOD draw:

- **shades a parent from its bake**, sampled at the pixel's position in the tile's sample grid —
  by `texelFetch` and a hand-written bilinear in sample space, not a LINEAR sampler. With f = 0
  the blend `(1 − f)·a + f·b` is `a` to the bit, so "the texel at this vertex" is exact, where a
  hardware filter returns it to within a fixed-point weight step that differs between GPUs
  (ADR-0066's m19.7b seam bound is that effect);
- **fades every tile toward its parent's bake** by the per-vertex geomorph factor, which
  `terrain.vert` now outputs (`v_lod.z`) — a level-0 tile from its full detail appearance, a
  parent from its own bake. `(1 − m)·own + m·parent`, so at m = 1 the tile shades exactly as its
  parent would. The parent's bake is bound for the child's draw (bindings 11/12; 9/10 are the
  tile's own).

**Why the geometry's factor and not a distance recomputed per pixel.** The two must agree at the
endpoints, not approximately: at m = 1 the vertex stage has put the surface on the parent's, and
the picture stops popping only if the shading is the parent's at that same moment. One number,
computed once and used for both, cannot disagree with itself — including on an edge the vertex
stage *forced* to 1 because the neighbour is coarser, which no distance formula knows about.

Everything stays behind the existing BRDF and sky path, unchanged. The push block is still
208 bytes: the bake flags and the quadrant ride in `lod_tile.z` (bits 5–8).

### 5. Residency and the refusal policy

A parent whose manifest line names a bake loads its two textures with its heightfield and is not
resident until they have arrived or failed — it is never shown first in the placeholder. A child's
draw reads its parent's texture, so it is recorded as a reader of the parent's slot too: without
that mark the fence rule (ADR-0069) would have a hole the size of one borrowed texture.

**Appearance never costs coverage.** Each shortfall draws and is counted:

| situation | drawn | counter |
| --- | --- | --- |
| parent with no bake in the manifest (cooked without `palette_dir`, or over a palette-less tile) | placeholder material | `fallback_appearance_draws` |
| a bake texture fails to load | placeholder | `bake_load_failures`, then `fallback_appearance_draws` |
| a bake of the wrong format or size | placeholder | `bake_refusals` (and the pass's `bakes_refused`) |
| a tile whose parent has no bake or is not resident | no fade | `parent_bake_missing_draws` |

For a fully cooked world all four stay 0. A level-0 world, and every non-LOD draw, take exactly
the instructions they took before.

## A choice beyond the spec: corner bits

`TerrainLodLeaf::coarser_corners` — a tile that touches a coarser one **only at a corner** holds
that vertex at morph 1. Its two same-level neighbours have the coarse tile across an edge, so
8d2's edge bits already force the vertex to 1 in them; the diagonal tile would otherwise fade it by
distance alone and shade the shared vertex differently. The geometry never needed this — a corner
is a parent vertex and does not move — which is why 8d2 did not have it.

It is load-bearing only when a tile is coarser because its children are *not resident* while the
camera is near: where a tile is coarser because it is far, every vertex touching it is already
past the finer level's morph range (measured: the whole camera sweep passes with the bits
removed). So the proof constructs the other case on purpose.

**The sRGB decode bound is half a code, not a constant.** The first version of (a) asserted
ADR-0066's flat 2e-3 and failed on the 3060 by up to 4.1e-3 — at bright texels only. Read back
per texel, the GPU's decode of byte 251 is 0.9609, where the IEC curve gives 0.9647: half an 8-bit
code, in the *encoded* space, which is the tolerance the conversion rules allow. In linear light
that is the curve's slope times half a code — 1.5e-4 near black, 4.5e-3 near white — so the bound
is derived per texel. This is the GPU's decode, not the bake: shared edges and the switch compare
two fetches of the same bytes and come out bit-identical.

## Consequences and known limits

- A coarse tile now looks like the ground beneath it, and a level switch changes no pixel.
- **The bake knows a layer by its mean.** Mid-morph, a level-0 tile is a cross-fade between its
  textured detail and a smooth average; at morph 1 the detail is gone. That is a fade, not a pop,
  but it is visible as a loss of texture with distance. A detail-preserving far appearance (a
  macro texture, or baking through the real layer textures at the parent's texel density) is not
  here.
- **Cross-level continuity relies on the nesting.** A fine tile's border shows its parent's bake;
  the coarse neighbour shows its own, and they are the same texels only while the coarse tile is
  not itself morphing there. Measured 0 violations over the sweep, ideal and forced fallback; not
  proven for every fallback pattern.
- **A bake's CPU copy is never released**: textures are the AssetServer's retained kind
  (ADR-0067 §5), so every parent ever loaded keeps 8·N² bytes until the server dies.
- **Cook memory** is 40 bytes per level-0 sample of the world, held at once; unmeasured at real
  sizes. Cook time is O(levels × world samples).
- **A block-compressed layer texture is not averaged**: the cook reads RGBA8 sRGB only, and a
  palette naming a BC7-textured layer is refused (`UnresolvedLayer`).
- Up to 16 texel fetches per pixel for a morphing parent (own + parent bake, colour + material);
  unmeasured.
- The bake ignores slope: layer textures are projected along world Y (ADR-0066), and so is this.
- Colour above 1.0 (a base-colour factor over 1) is clamped by the sRGB encode.

## What 8e must do

- Measure: bake bytes per slot and the fetch cost above; move bakes to the streamed asset path so
  their CPU copies are released (the byte budget needs it).
- Decide whether a parent should wait for its bake under pressure (it does now; unmeasured with
  slow IO — `bake_waits` counts it).
- Frustum culling, batching and the range tuning ADR-0071 already listed.
