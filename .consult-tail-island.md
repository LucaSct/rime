# Consult: how to pay down a 5.3 ms single-island solve tail (2026-10-12)

**Asked:** `gpt-6-astra` via `codex exec`, `model_reasoning_effort=high`, `--sandbox read-only`,
one self-contained prompt with the measurement inlined and **no repo access** (deliberate: I wanted
its own proposal, not a reading of our code). Prompt kept in the repo at
`.consult-tail-island-prompt.md`; full answer at `.consult-tail-island-astra.md` (both copied out of
the session scratchpad, which is tmpfs and dies with a reboot).
~35 k tokens. Review that actually ran on the conclusion below: **astra consulted, me deciding.**

## What it proposed, its own ranking

1. **Cut the iteration budget 8/2 -> 4/1.** Minutes to implement; the validation is the cost.
   Estimate `T_new = T/2 + H/2` for non-iteration overhead `H`. Quality: less load propagation
   through stacks, softer/slippier contacts, different collapse timing. Determinism fine.
2. **Hoist genuinely invariant per-constraint work out of the 8 velocity passes** — effective mass,
   world-space inertia transforms, lever arms, tangent basis, material combine, index lookup.
   Quality-neutral if the cached values really are invariant; explicitly NOT to be extended to NGS,
   whose whole point is re-reading the rotated pose. "If preparation is already good, this
   candidate has little headroom."
3. **Deterministic graph-coloured intra-island solving** (Box2D v3's shape). Days to weeks. Keeps
   Gauss-Seidel between colours, so better than Jacobi, but ordering changes convergence; 1348
   constraints may give batches too small to beat the barriers.

It would **not** start by sleeping individual slow bodies, and gave a physical reason worth keeping.

## Where I agree

- **The sleep/split lever is not next, and its objection is sharper than mine was.** A stationary
  body can be carrying the building's load; freezing it individually either invents a support or
  loses support propagation, and a correct wake rule would re-wake the whole connected component
  anyway — which is the thing we were trying to avoid. I had been treating "most of the island is
  below the sleep threshold" as headroom; it is only headroom if those bodies are not load paths,
  and in a collapsing building they are exactly the load paths.
- **Candidate 2 is the right shape of lever** — pay once, change nothing physical.
- **Its third framing correction is a real gap in our instrument:** the worst solve STEP need not be
  in the tick that sets `sim.block` p99, so "halve the step" does not imply "p99 drops by half of
  it". Folded into the phase-split brick as an explicit same-tick check.

## Where I disagree, and which side I take

- **I reject its ranking of #1 first.** Effort is not the ordering that matters here; reversibility
  is. `99-the-block` exists to show a building that stands and then collapses believably, and the
  breach is 42 %, not 2 % — so the first lever tried should be one that cannot change the picture.
  4/1 iterations trades the exact property the sample is a demo of, and astra itself says the
  validation (penetration, stack drift, collapse progression, sleeping) is the expensive part. So
  **quality-neutral work comes first, and the iteration cut becomes the fallback we price against a
  measured per-iteration cost** — which is why the phase-split brick reports `velocity_ns / 8`.
- **Its "little headroom if preparation is good" caveat does not apply here, and I can name why** —
  see below. This is the part a model with no repo access could not reach, and it is the one place
  its answer was wrong in substance rather than in emphasis.

## What I verified in our code afterwards (MEASURED from source, not inferred)

- **`prepare_contact_constraint` already caches almost everything on astra's list** — `normal_mass`,
  `tangent_mass[2]`, `velocity_bias`, the tangent basis, the lever arms `r_a`/`r_b`, combined
  friction, combined restitution, all once per manifold per step (`engine/physics/src/solver.hpp`
  :216). So most of candidate 2 is already done, exactly as astra guessed it might be.
- **One thing is NOT cached, and it is recomputed in the innermost loop.** `apply_impulse`
  (`solver.hpp`:177) calls `apply_inv_inertia(orientation, inertia_principal, inv_inertia, r x p)`
  per body, and that overload composes `q * principal` and then does **two quaternion rotates**, on
  every call. The comment above it (`solver.hpp`:108) says the compose is *"DELIBERATELY not cached
  per tick: the NGS position pass rotates bodies mid-pass and re-reads their orientation"*. **That
  reason is true of `solve_positions` and false of `solve_velocities`:** nothing in
  `solve_velocities` writes `orientation` — the integration loop that does runs *after* it, in
  `solve_island`. So across all 8 velocity iterations each body's `q`, its composite `q * principal`
  and its world inverse inertia are **constant**, and we recompute them per impulse application.
- **Call-count upper bound (INFERRED, from 1348 manifolds x <= 4 points):** the velocity phase makes
  up to `1348 x 4 x 8 iterations x 3 impulses x 2 bodies ~= 2.6e5` `apply_inv_inertia` calls, i.e. up
  to ~2.6e5 quaternion composes and ~5.2e5 quaternion rotates, for a quantity with **362** distinct
  values that do not change. The exact point count is not instrumented — `IslandSolveCost.constraints`
  counts MANIFOLDS, not points (`world.cpp`:1278 pushes one constraint per manifold), and the
  header comment calling them "(points)" is wrong and should be fixed.
- **Astra's second framing correction is already satisfied here:** static/kinematic bodies are
  shared anchors and not island members (`world.cpp`:1284 comment), and a pair with no dynamic
  member never becomes a constraint (`world.cpp`:1266). So the ground does **not** union otherwise
  independent dynamic components, and the 362-body island is a genuinely connected dynamic
  structure rather than an artefact of the partition. That also means there is no cheap structural
  fix hiding there — the island is real.

## The plan this produces

1. **Now, in flight:** `perf/island-phase-split` — per-phase timing (`warm` / `velocity` / `store` /
   `position`) inside one island's solve, under the existing `--island-profile` flag, plus the
   same-tick check astra asked for. Pure instrument; sizes every candidate below in one run.
2. **Then, if `velocity_ns` dominates as expected:** hoist the composed orientation out of the
   velocity sweep. Two tiers, and the tier matters:
   - **tier 1, bit-identical:** cache `q * principal` per body for the velocity phase only. The
     value is identical, so every rotate downstream is identical, so `world_hash()` does not move
     and no golden hash is re-baselined. Saves one quaternion product of the three operations.
   - **tier 2, faster but re-baselines the hash:** build the symmetric world inverse inertia 3x3
     once per body per step and replace the two rotates with a matvec (~15 flops against ~45). It
     is mathematically the same operator but not the same rounding, so replay hashes move once.
   Leave NGS on the live-orientation path either way — that is what the existing comment is right
   about, and the comment needs correcting to say *velocity phase* rather than *per tick*.
3. **Only then** price the 4/1 iteration cut against the measured per-iteration cost, as a
   deliberate quality trade with Luca's call, not as an optimisation.
4. **Graph colouring stays on the shelf** behind both, with astra's caveat recorded: at 1348
   manifolds the colour batches may be too small to beat the barriers, so it needs its own
   measurement before it is worth days.

## How the output landed

**Useful, and wrong in one place I could check.** Its ranking was effort-first where this project
needs reversibility-first, and its "preparation is probably already good" hedge would have closed
the one door that is actually open. The two framing corrections were worth the call on their own:
one is now a check in the instrument, the other I could refute from the source in two minutes and
now have written down so it is not re-asked.
